# 6.2 UAT and defect reporting

The business-facing end of testing. UAT is owned by the business owner rather than QA, and is written in business language with no technical vocabulary at all; the defect template is what keeps triage from turning into a reproduce-it-yourself exercise.

The engineering-facing plan and cases are in [6.1](6.1-test-plan.md).

## Contents

- [UAT](#uat)
- [Bug reports](#bug-reports)

## UAT

UAT is written for business users, in business language, with no technical vocabulary at all. Each scenario is a task the user actually does, phrased as an outcome, with space to record what happened.

| # | Scenario | Steps | Expected outcome | Result | Tester | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Include the sign-off block: who accepts, on what date, and what conditions are attached. Record conditional acceptances explicitly ("accepted subject to defect #142 being fixed before go-live") — verbal conditions get forgotten and then get disputed.

## Bug reports

A consistent template saves the reproduce-it-yourself round trip that consumes most triage time.

```markdown
**Title** — <what is wrong, in one line>

| | |
|---|---|
| **Severity** | 1 Critical / 2 Major / 3 Minor / 4 Cosmetic |
| **Environment** | staging, build 2026.8.3, Chrome 128 |
| **Related** | TC-CO-014, R7 |

**Steps to reproduce**
1.
2.

**Expected** — 
**Actual** — 
**Evidence** — logs, screenshot, request ID, correlation ID
**Frequency** — always / intermittent (n of m attempts)
```

Define the severity scale once in the test plan so it means the same thing to everyone: severity describes impact on the user, priority describes urgency of the fix, and they are set by different people. Conflating them is why "critical" bugs sometimes sit untouched for a week.
